Five problems I could not stop noticing.

And what I built for each. Jessie Chen, 陈亦奇, AI product manager candidate, University of Chicago MA in Computational Social Science, class of 2027. Five AI products shipped solo in the past year; three run in production every day.

Products and case pages are in Chinese; this page is in English. Numbers read 2026-09.

PROBLEM 01

In class, the question dies before you finish typing it.

Online lectures. Noticed in my own notes.

So I built Lecture Companion.

The bottleneck is input friction, not model capability. A question became one click on a transcript sentence; multimodal retrieval fills in the whiteboard and formulas. The North Star is after-class review, not in-class activity.

PROBLEM 02

You save everything and never go back.

Xiaohongshu bookmarks. Mine, and everyone's.

So I built xhs-threads.

Organizing never happens because it needs the user to start. Here the user only saves. The AI threads bookmarks into trains of thought, writes the notes, and an independent reviewer model checks the output before anything is published.

PROBLEM 03

You say yes to things you will not do.

Group chats. Every week.

So I built Nous.

Other bots learn how you talk; Nous learns how you decide. A nine-dimension cognitive model judges whether to reply, drafts in your way, and raises a warning when you are about to agree to something you will not follow through on. Feishu bot, Mac floating assistant, Web.

PROBLEM 04

The job search is blocked by people, not information.

My own search, and my friends'.

So I built JobPilot.

Existing tools stop at the behavior layer. JobPilot plugs in a cognitive model and language samples to give a personal diagnosis and messaging that sounds like you. Open source; all six product trade-offs have a written why.

PROBLEM 05

The data is public and nobody reads it.

Shanghai musical-theatre ticketing. I was already in the audience.

So I built MusiClaw.

Public ticketing data turned into a product that updates itself daily: seat-map snapshots, cast attribution analysis, four content pipelines feeding a real data account. What the AI may and may not decide is written down.

How I pick a problem.

Pain point before solution.

Find the layer the bottleneck lives in before building. Wrong layer, no feature fixes it.

Staged rollout, attribution.

One layer of change per stage, so a problem points at its own layer.

Metric before number.

Marks reviewed after class, not made in class. Every number names its dashboard and read date.

Cost is a product parameter.

Every call logged and routed by tier; a shadow run proves quality before cost is cut.
