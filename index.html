Design, Build, Ship · Assignment 1 · Progress 25 / 25

One personal landing page, 25 versions

One idea, content frozen, only the form changes. The visitor is a hiring manager for an AI product role. After reading they should understand how I think about and ship products, then open one project or send an email. v01–v10 go wide, v11–v18 narrow to two or three lines and combine them, v19–v25 converge on one line, each version a visible step.

Starting point I already have a live personal site (jessieyiqichen.github.io): paper background, serif headings, numbered sections, five projects laid out as evidence. This assignment questions whether that form is right. It is one candidate, not the answer.

Act 1 · Go wide v01–v10 · each version changes one axis: layout / audience / mood / era / medium metaphor / visitor action

v01
Interactive résumé + guide
A résumé on grey paper, serif throughout, one blue. Every entry opens to why it is there, a screenshot and links; my memoji offers a seven-stop tour and walks beside each entry. Changed from the first try: dropped the white sheet, sans-serif and mono labels that read as AI output; added the bilingual name and a thumbnail on every row. Two earlier v01s (a ledger, then a plain single column) were dropped: one stopped reading as a person, the other gave the visitor nothing to do.
v02
Choose your lens
Changes the visitor action: the same five products, read through one of three lenses the visitor picks: product (the problem chosen, the metric picked), engineering (how it was built, shipped and kept running), or data and cost (the numbers and what each call costs). Switching re-colours the page, rewrites every card’s claim and headline number, re-sorts the five so the strongest under that lens comes first, and lights the working rules that lens leans on. All facts from the same source, only regrouped. Question: does letting a hiring manager pick what to check beat one fixed story? Skin after a reference, signatures left behind: near-black with grain, one statement per screen, the five screenshots on a prism that turns as you scroll (CSS 3D, no library), and each card carrying a hand-drawn white line diagram of the product’s flow. Schibsted Grotesk and Martian Mono; monochrome, with yellow, blue or green lighting only the active lens. First try: a 30-second or full-read switch, which changed how the page opened but not what the visitor did. Replaces the first v02 (a name wall of ink-dot letters on near-black), late: the lens idea came up in act 2 but it is a new direction, not a combination, so it belongs here.
v03
Manual
Changes the audience: written for a technical interviewer who wants to check, not browse. Styled as a printed product manual: IBM Plex Sans and Mono, oxide red on warm grey, vertical chapter numerals in the margin, index tabs on the right edge, two columns with a figure and a sourced data table per chapter. Changed from the first try: a plain report layout that read as a default document.
v04
Academic home page
Changes the era: a 1990s academic personal page, the form many professors still use. Times, black on white, default link blue and visited purple, a photo table at the top, and the five products written as a publication list with [case study] [demo] [figure] links. No styling budget at all; the content has to carry it.
v05
Pipeline canvas
Changes the visitor action: explore instead of read. A draggable canvas of nodes wired together, like an automation editor. I am the trigger, the four rules are switches that filter which products light up, each product shows its real pipeline with the reviewer model or cost ledger as a node, details open in a dialog over the canvas, and the output node is contact. System sans and Plex Mono, amber for whatever is live.
v06
Typed
Changes the visitor action again, and the tone: three short sentences, "I build / I measure / I am", each with one green word. Pull a word and the rest of the sentence is typed out in front of you, new lines appear, products get a thumbnail. Nothing types until you ask. Courier Prime on pale typing paper, one green. A "plain version" switch shows everything at once.
v07
Scrapbook
Changes the composition and the subject: the person first, the work inside the story. A kraft-paper scrapbook of real material, my own photos (Wuhan, Hong Kong, Shanghai, Chicago, the dog, the cat, the Playbills), screenshots as clippings, numbers on sticky notes, rubber stamps, tape, a Chinese seal. Everything rotated and overlapping, which agents never do. Courier Prime, Source Serif and a heavy sans mixed, as a scrapbook would.
v08
Spec sheet
Changes the image treatment and the tone: a product spec sheet for a person. My photo rendered as a halftone of black dots on grey paper, the real photo showing only around the cursor; a nameplate of coordinates for Wuhan, Hong Kong, Shanghai and Chicago; the five products as a real table with year, role and figures, each row opening in place. Archivo for text, Plex Mono only on the plate. Two earlier v08s were dropped: a 120px poster that looked generated, and a plain booklet that had no character.
v09
One screen
Changes the container: nothing scrolls. One screen with the memoji, one line of who I am, one claim, and a dock of eight icons: five products as emoji on soft tints, then email, résumé and GitHub. Each opens a panel built to one spec, header with the icon, problem / what I built / results, link buttons, previous and next. About lives behind a small pill at the top. System font. Changed along the way: hand-drawn tile icons replaced by emoji after they looked clumsy; the first panels were plain boxes.
v10
A letter with attachments
Changes the voice: the page is a cover letter addressed to the hiring manager, first person, dated. Each product is mentioned in the text as an attachment; clicking the name opens that attachment in a tray on the right, with the screenshot, the numbers and the links. The tray starts as a list of five thumbnails so the work is visible before reading. Encl. line at the end, as on a real letter. Alegreya on white, ink navy.

What act 1 found. The versions that held were the ones with real screenshots, a real person and warm, physical materials; dashboards, terminals and abstract effects read as one more AI-made page.

Act 2 · Narrow v11–v18 · keep two or three lines, start combining

v11
A résumé on the desk
Recipe: content and question from the dropped v11 (a résumé with direct evidence), the physical objects from v07, a real two-column résumé layout instead of v01’s single column. A white sheet lies on a slate desk; each product has its screenshot paper-clipped beside it as a print hanging off the edge of the page. Pick a print up and it fills the screen; turn it over for the numbers and links. Personal photos moved off the page onto the desk below, with a note card. Question: is the most familiar format enough once the evidence is physically attached to it? EB Garamond, slate felt, one rust red. Earlier attempts: a single-column résumé that read as a v01 revision, and before that landscape sheets slid sideways.
v12
Person first

Earlier v12s: a long letter with panels, a grey one-screen note, an ink-night note (too abstract).
v13
Scrapbook spread, work first
Recipe: v09’s one screen, v13’s work first, and v07’s scrapbook. Nothing scrolls: an open scrapbook on kraft pages. Earlier v13s: rough rings, a generic list, a pinned column, an app window with a dock (too much like software), a film contact sheet (too familiar), then a spread with me on the left page (the work got lost).
v14
Typed, with the evidence pinned
Recipe: v06’s typing and its choose-your-path reading, on a new layout. The left half is a typed letter; only three sentences are there at first, each with red words. Click one and the page types that thread out. Whenever the typing reaches a product, its screenshot drops onto the board on the right as a pinned print (the physical prints of v11); the rules and a photo of me pin there too. By the end each reader has built their own board of the evidence they chose. Click a print to see it up close; a button shows everything at once. Question: does choosing what to read make a hiring manager read more? Cutive Mono in a two-colour typewriter ribbon, black and blue, white paper, grey board.
v15
Five names, with a guide
Recipe: a pattern found while surveying portfolios (oversized project names that swap the background on hover), the act 1 idea that the page takes its colour only from the products, and the memoji guide of v01. The page is black and white: five names in a large serif. Point at one and its screenshot fills the screen in colour, a hand-drawn underline draws itself, and my memoji walks over to that row and says, in the first person, one line and one number about it; click the name for the case. The guide gives a work-first page a person. Question: does a guide make five names enough to choose from? Young Serif and Libre Franklin, warm grey and ink. Tried in between and set aside: a grey spec table with a colour reveal, then a magazine contents page; both moved further from the version v16 is built on.

Earlier v17s: a dark list of large names with lens buttons (looked like v15), then a dark index-and-screen layout (looked like a software dashboard).
v18
The person, then the drawer
The end of act 2: v16 and v17 joined, each covering the other’s weakness. v16’s frame stays (the letter’s opening beside a real photo, a memoji and one sentence about me, doodles, how I work, off-hours photos, Titi and Damai, a coffee), but its dark list of names, which broke the warm page in two, is replaced by v17’s file drawer: five pastel cards, lens dividers that re-file them, a peek on hover and a card pulled out on click. What act 2 found. Combinations that looked like software (a window, a spec table, a master–detail list) were dropped; the ones built from paper objects stayed. v18, the person-first letter with the file drawer, became the one line to refine.

v24
Hiring for what?
Changes the visitor's first action. Until v23 the lenses waited on the second screen, so most visitors read the product angle by default. The old headline, five AI products from a blank page to launch, moves to the line beneath. Here the headline itself is the question: are you hiring for product, engineering, or data? Each of the three words is a button in its lens colour; one tap dims the other two, adds a line saying how the page is now filed, files the drawer by that lens, pulls out the product that leads it, and scrolls there; "or just see the five" stays for anyone who does not want to choose. A phone-only version was considered for this slot and dropped: graders see the gallery on a desktop, where it would look the same as v23.
v25
The letter, signed
Closes the letter the page opens. The top bar repeated my name beside the name card; it is now a letterhead, the date on the left and the links on the right, so the name appears once. The bottom asked "Have questions?"; it is now a sign-off: Yours, my name, the email written large, Résumé and GitHub, and a P.S. that every product links to a demo. From "Dear hiring manager" to the signature the page now reads as one letter. Also fixed for phones: an opened card is one column and nothing scrolls sideways.

What act 3 found. Adding one object per version (a board, a binder, a calling card, a scrapbook) improved each part and crowded the page. v22 took them away and kept one material; v23 to v25 refined that page in steps a visitor can see.

Final choice v25. It reads as one letter to the person it is for: it opens with "Dear hiring manager", asks what they are hiring for and files the five products by that answer, pulls the one that leads out of the drawer with a guided walk through its screen, and signs off with my name, email and résumé. Its line runs from v12 (person first) and v17 (the file drawer), joined in v18. From the later tries it kept only what helped the reader: the name card (v21), the marker rings and the guided walk (v13, v15), the lenses (v02). Dropped on the way: the board, the binder and the scrapbook, each good alone, together too many materials for one page.
