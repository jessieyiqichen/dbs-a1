Design, Build, Ship · Assignment 1 · Progress 25 / 25

One personal landing page, 25 versions

One idea, content frozen, only the form changes. The visitor is a hiring manager for an AI product role. After reading they should understand how I think about and ship products, then open one project or send an email. v01–v10 go wide, v11–v18 narrow to two or three lines and combine them, v19–v25 converge on one line, each version a visible step.

Starting point I already have a live personal site (jessieyiqichen.github.io): paper background, serif headings, numbered sections, five projects laid out as evidence. This assignment questions whether that form is right. It is one candidate, not the answer.

Act 1 · Go wide v01–v10 · each version changes one axis: layout / audience / mood / era / medium metaphor / visitor action

v01
Interactive résumé + guide
A résumé on grey paper, serif throughout, one blue. Each of the five products opens to why it is there, a screenshot and links; my memoji offers a seven-stop tour and, on a wide window, walks beside each entry. The name is in English and Chinese, and every product row has a thumbnail.
v02
Choose your lens
Changes the visitor action: the same five products, read through one of three lenses the visitor picks: product (the problem chosen, the metric picked), engineering (how it was built, shipped and kept running), or data and cost (the numbers and what each call costs). Switching re-colours the page, rewrites every card’s claim and headline number, re-sorts the five so the strongest under that lens comes first, and lights the working rules that lens leans on. All facts from the same source, only regrouped. Question: does letting a hiring manager pick what to check beat one fixed story? Skin after a reference, signatures left behind: near-black with grain, one statement per screen, the five screenshots on a prism that turns as you scroll (CSS 3D, no library), and each card carrying a hand-drawn white line diagram of the product’s flow. Schibsted Grotesk and Martian Mono; monochrome apart from the lenses, one yellow, one blue, one green, and the page takes the colour of the lens chosen. Made after act 2: the lens idea is a new direction, not a combination, so it sits in act 1.
v03
Manual
Changes the audience: written for a technical interviewer who wants to check, not browse. Styled as a printed product manual: IBM Plex Sans and Mono, oxide red on warm grey, vertical chapter numerals in the margin, index tabs on the right edge, two columns with a figure and a sourced data table per chapter.
v04
Academic home page
Changes the era: a 1990s academic personal page, the form many professors still use. Times, black on white, default link blue and visited purple, a table at the top with my memoji beside my name and affiliation, and the five products written as a publication list with bracketed links: [case study], [demo], [figure] and the like. Almost no styling; the content has to carry it.
v05
Pipeline canvas
Changes the visitor action: explore instead of read. A draggable canvas of nodes wired together, like an automation editor. I am the trigger, the four rules are switches that filter which products light up, each product node lists its real pipeline steps (a reviewer model, a usage ledger, a test suite), details open in a dialog over the canvas, and the output node is contact. System sans and Plex Mono, with amber for the node headers and the steps that matter.
v06
Typed
Changes the visitor action again, and the tone: three short sentences, "I build / I measure / I am", each with one green word. Pull a word and the rest of the sentence is typed out in front of you, new lines appear, products get a thumbnail. Nothing types until you ask. Courier Prime on pale typing paper, one green. A "plain version" button shows everything at once.
v07
Scrapbook
Changes the composition and the subject: the person first, the work inside the story. A kraft-paper scrapbook of real material, my own photos (an ice cave, Christmas 2005, Wuhan, Hong Kong, Chicago, the dogs and cats, the Playbills), screenshots as clippings, tape, a rubber stamp and a Chinese seal. Almost everything is rotated and overlapping, which agents never do. Courier Prime, Source Serif and a heavy sans mixed, as a scrapbook would.
v08
Spec sheet
Changes the image treatment and the tone: a product spec sheet for a person. My photo rendered in black dither on grey paper, the real photo showing only around the cursor; a nameplate of coordinates for Wuhan, Hong Kong, Shanghai and Chicago; the five products as a real table with year, name, one line and figures, each row opening in place. Archivo for text, Plex Mono for labels and figures.
v09
One screen
Changes the container: nothing scrolls. One screen with the memoji, one line of who I am, one claim, and a dock of eight icons: five products as emoji on soft tints, then email, résumé and GitHub. Each product opens a panel built to one spec, header with the icon, problem / what I built / results, link buttons, previous and next; email, résumé and GitHub go straight there. About lives behind a small pill at the top. System font.
v10
A letter with attachments
Changes the voice: the page is a cover letter addressed to the hiring manager, first person, dated. Each product is mentioned in the text as an attachment; clicking the name opens that attachment in a tray on the right, with the screenshot, the numbers and the links. The tray starts as a list of five thumbnails so the work is visible before reading. Encl. line at the end, as on a real letter. Alegreya on white, ink navy.

What act 1 found. The versions that held were the ones with real screenshots, a real person and warm, physical materials; the software-like ones, such as v05’s canvas, read as one more AI-made page.

Act 2 · Narrow v11–v18 · keep two or three lines, start combining

v11
A résumé on the desk
Recipe: a résumé with the evidence attached, the physical objects from v07, and a real two-column résumé layout instead of v01’s single column. A white sheet lies on a slate desk; each product has its screenshot paper-clipped beside it as a print hanging off the edge of the page. Pick a print up and it comes up large; turn it over for the numbers and links. Personal photos moved off the page onto the desk below, with a note card. Question: is the most familiar format enough once the evidence is physically attached to it? EB Garamond, slate felt, one rust red.
v12
Person first
Recipe: a real photo of me as the device (from v07), the letter’s voice (from v10), and an editorial layout from a reference site, with its signatures left behind. Split first screen: the letter’s opening on the left, the ice-cave photo full height on the right. Then a memoji and one sentence about who I am, a dark block where three short hiring-manager questions sit around a screenshot and are answered beside it, a two-column grid of the five products in colour with a case panel each, a last cell for questions, and an off-hours strip. Seven small hand-drawn line doodles in ink and mustard (a gondola, a ticket, the Chicago skyline, a notebook with a rising line, a coffee, Titi and Damai) add the warmth the reference got from illustration, drawn for this page rather than borrowed. Question: if the visitor sees the person first, do they read further? Newsreader headings over a system sans, cool white, deep spruce, one mustard.
v13
Scrapbook spread, work first
Recipe: v09’s one screen and v07’s scrapbook, with the work put first. Nothing scrolls: an open scrapbook on kraft pages. The whole left page is one product, taped in large: the screen (hover for a loupe, click to light its three notes in turn), the name, the quoted result, the notes, the numbers and links. The right page holds the other four as smaller prints; click one and it flies across, straightens and becomes the open product. Below them, smaller, is me: name, memoji, a strip of six photos (Wuhan, Christmas 2005, Hong Kong, Chicago, Titi, Damai), education and contact. While MusiClaw is among the prints, the Playbills sit beside it, the habit behind it. Question, against v12: can one spread put the work first and still be fun? Barlow Condensed caps and Hanken Grotesk, kraft, marker blue.
v14
Typed, with the evidence pinned
Recipe: v06’s typing and its choose-your-path reading, on a new layout. The left half is a typed letter; only three sentences are there at first, each with blue words. Click one and the page types that thread out. Whenever the typing reaches a product, its screenshot drops onto the board on the right as a pinned print (the physical prints of v11); the rules and a photo of me pin there too. By the end each reader has built their own board of the evidence they chose. Click a print to see it up close; a button shows everything at once. Question: does choosing what to read make a hiring manager read more? Cutive Mono in a two-colour typewriter ribbon, black and blue, white paper, grey board.
v15
Five names, with a guide
Recipe: a pattern found while surveying portfolios (oversized project names that swap the background on hover), the act 1 idea that the page takes its colour only from the products, and the memoji guide of v01. The page is black and white: five names in a large serif. Point at one and its screenshot fills the screen in colour, a hand-drawn underline draws itself, and my memoji walks over to that row and says, in the first person, one line and one number about it; click the name for the case. The guide gives a work-first page a person. Question: does a guide make five names enough to choose from? Young Serif and Libre Franklin, warm grey and ink.
v16
The person, then the five
First convergence: the two parts I responded to most, joined. The top half is v12: a split first screen with the letter’s opening and a real photo, a memoji and one sentence about who I am, the hand-drawn doodles. Then the page turns dark and becomes v15’s list without its guide: five large names, each filling the section with its screenshot in colour on hover, a hand-drawn underline, one sentence and one number, a case on click. It ends warm again with how I work, photos, Titi and Damai, and a coffee. Question: does the person-then-work order hold together on one page? Young Serif and Libre Franklin, warm paper, near-black, mustard.
v17
The file drawer
Second convergence, the counterpart to v16: work first, and the visitor picks the angle. The five are a stack of coloured cards in a file drawer, after a reference that stacked its projects as cards (its giant name and tagline left behind). The lenses of v02 become folder dividers: pick Product, Engineering or Data and cost and the cards re-file in that order, while the divider says what the order means. Point at a card and it lifts to show one line and one number for that lens; click and it is pulled out to show the screenshot, the lens’s paragraph, three numbers and links. Question, against v16: warm and person-first, or work-first with a choice? Young Serif and Libre Franklin on paper; pastel cards, yellow, blue and green tabs.
v18
The person, then the drawer
The end of act 2: v16 and v17 joined, each covering the other’s weakness. v16’s frame stays (the letter’s opening beside a real photo, a memoji and one sentence about me, doodles, how I work, off-hours photos, Titi and Damai, a coffee), but its dark list of names, which broke the warm page in two, is replaced by v17’s file drawer: five pastel cards, lens dividers that re-file them, a peek on hover and a card pulled out on click. Each card now carries its own hand-drawn doodle (a blackboard, bookmarks, two speech bubbles, a funnel, a ticket), so the stack reads at a glance. The whole page stays on paper. This is the line act 3 refines. Young Serif and Libre Franklin; paper, pastel cards, ink and mustard doodles.

What act 2 found. The combinations that held were built from paper objects, not software: prints on a desk, a scrapbook spread, a typed letter with a pinboard, a file drawer. v18, the person-first letter with the file drawer, became the one line to refine.

Act 3 · Converge v19–v25 · one line left, each version a visible step

v19
The work on the first screen
Act 3 refines v18, one decision per version. This one changes what the first screen shows. In v18 the right half of the first screen was my photo and the work started a scroll later; here it is a product card already pulled out of the drawer: Nous, its screen, one line and its number. Clicking it scrolls to the drawer and opens Nous there. My photo moves down beside the sentence about who I am, as a print. Everything else is v18.
v20
The five, pinned on a board
Changes the first screen again, from one product to all five. v19 showed one card, Nous; the other four waited a scroll below. Here the right half is a kraft board with five screenshots taped on as prints: Nous large with a slip carrying its line and number, the other four small in a row. Clicking a small print flies it into the large spot and sends the large one back to the row, and the slip rewrites itself, so the five can be compared without leaving the first screen; only the large print, or "Read the full case", scrolls to the drawer. The change sits on the first screen, so v20 and v19 differ at first glance. Rest is v19.
v21
Binder and calling card
Two additions, made one after the other in this slot. First, how a case opens: the drawer became a ring binder, one product per spread, the screenshot taped on the left with three spots ringed in blue marker and numbered, a loupe on hover, the line, numbers and links on the right; index tabs turn the page and the lenses became sticky notes. Then, where the person is: as a portfolio the page hid who I am, so a calling card was taped under the headline, with the ice-cave photo, name, AI Product · Chicago / Shanghai, the degree, the email written out, Résumé and GitHub. Both worked alone; together with the board they made too many materials, which v22 answers.
v22
Name card and drawer
Takes things away. By v21 the page had a kraft board, a taped card, a navy binder, sticky notes and index tabs: each part worked alone, together the first screen had no clear order. This version keeps one material, light paper and five pastel cards. The first screen is the letter's headline on the left and a large name card on the right: photo, name, AI Product · Chicago / Shanghai, the degree, the email written out, Résumé and GitHub. The board is gone, since the drawer just below already shows the five. Kept from v21: the name card's content and the numbered marker rings on an opened card's screen.
v23
The first one, already out
Changes the drawer's default state. v22 cleared the first screen, but its cost was that no screenshot showed until a visitor clicked: the closed drawer was five names. Here the drawer opens with its lead card already out, so the second screen is real work, the screenshot with its numbered marker rings, the line, the numbers and links. Switching lens files the cards again and pulls out the one that now leads: Nous for product, JobPilot for engineering, xhs-threads for data and cost. The guided walk from v13 and v15 comes back inside the open card: "Walk me through it" dims the screenshot except one ringed spot while my memoji explains it below, three spots then the number the product lives by, and "Next product →" carries on to the next card.
v24
Hiring for what?
Changes the visitor's first action. Until v23 the lenses waited on the second screen, so most visitors read the product angle by default. The old headline, five AI products from a blank page to launch, moves to the line beneath. Here the headline itself is the question: are you hiring for product, engineering, or data? Each of the three words is a button in its lens colour; one tap dims the other two, rewrites the line beneath to say how the page is now filed, files the drawer by that lens, pulls out the product that leads it, and scrolls there; "or just see the five" stays for anyone who does not want to choose.
v25
The letter, signed
Two changes. The one on the first screen: my memoji now waits in the corner and asks "Want a 40-second tour?". In v24 the walk stayed inside one opened card; here "Show me" walks the whole page in eight stops, from the question in the headline to the name card, the drawer, three ringed spots on a screen, the number and the signature, with everything but the current stop in shadow, and a card's "Walk me through it" joins the same walk at its screen. The other closes the letter the page opens: the top bar, which repeated my name beside the name card, is now a letterhead with the date; the bottom, which asked "Have questions?", is now a sign-off: Yours, my name, the email written large, Résumé and GitHub, and a P.S. that every product links to a demo. From "Dear hiring manager" to the signature the page reads as one letter. Also fixed for phones: an opened card is one column and nothing scrolls sideways.

What act 3 found. Adding objects (a board in v20, then a binder and a calling card in v21) improved each part and crowded the page. v22 took them away and kept one material; v23 to v25 refined that page in steps a visitor can see.

Final choice v25. It reads as one letter to the person it is for: it opens with "Dear hiring manager", asks what they are hiring for and files the five products by that answer, pulls the one that leads out of the drawer, lets my memoji walk them through the page, and signs off with my name, email and résumé. Its line runs from v12 (person first) and v17 (the file drawer), joined in v18. From the later tries it kept only what helped the reader: the name card and the marker rings (v21), the guided walk (v13, v15), the lenses (v02). Left behind on the way: the board and the binder, each good alone, together too many materials for one page.
